Photo guide

Clear, consistently numbered images make it much easier to build and check your coin inventory.

1. Photograph manageable groups

Place coins separately on a plain, contrasting surface. Photograph straight from above in good, even light. Avoid overlap, glare and deep shadows.

2. Keep every coin in the same position

When you turn a group over for the reverse image, keep each coin in exactly the same position. This is especially important when several coins appear in one image.

3. Number image pairs

Use simple matching names, for example 01-front and 01-reverse, followed by 02-front/02-reverse. Do not rely only on the automatic filename from your phone.

4. Check the pairing yourself

The Beta is intentionally simple. Always verify that the front and reverse shown for a coin belong together. Correct the link when necessary.

5. Add useful detail

If a date, mint mark or text is difficult to read, take an extra close-up. An edge image can also help for unusual coins.

Avoid

Overlapping coins, mixed front/reverse layouts, changing positions between image pairs, very distant shots, strong reflections, motion blur and important details hidden by fingers or holders.

Useful routine: photograph one group completely—front, reverse and optional details—before starting the next group. Keep the original images after exporting the inventory.
